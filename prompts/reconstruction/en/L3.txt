Core objective:
Beyond reproducing object categories and events, reproduce nearly completely the visible appearance, structure, actions, and imaging characteristics of the specific reference objects so that reconstruction and reference closely agree on observable content.

Modeling requirements:

1. Use detailed editable 3D models to accurately reproduce the specific objects' global and local silhouettes, proportions, structures, component shapes, and component distributions. Matching the category, adding polygons, or smoothing a placeholder is not a substitute for accurate reconstruction.
2. Accurately reproduce clearly visible appearance features that distinguish instances of the same category, including colors, patterns, textures, materials, and relevant local details.
3. Accurately reproduce visible global and local actions, pose changes, contacts, deformations, and secondary motion. Do not substitute generic actions or whole-object rigid motion for the specific visible actions.
4. Faithfully retain the reference style, whether live action, cartoon, game footage, or another style. Do not intentionally transform it into a different style.
5. The reconstruction target is observable reference content. Completing unseen regions must not change visible silhouettes, occlusions, lighting and shadows, or motion. Do not lower requirements for visible content because the video is monocular or contains occluded or unseen regions.

Execution requirements:

1. Actually inspect and edit the scene through Blender MCP; do not provide only a plan or code.
2. Analyze the reference video independently. You may use local video, image, and other auxiliary tools; choose your own analysis method and reference preparation.
3. Accurately preserve the number and persistent correspondence of main subjects, spatial and occlusion relations, support and contact, entries and exits, motion directions, starts and stops, turns, interactions, and event order.
4. Match the reference frame rate, frame count, duration, and timeline.
5. Camera-induced composition, perspective, image motion, subject positions and sizes in the image, and shot transitions must align nearly completely with the reference. Do not replace necessary camera changes with incorrect subject translation or scaling.
6. Construct the scene from editable 3D objects. Do not use the original video or reference frames as the final image, background, or projected image surfaces in place of 3D reconstruction.
7. Render with Cycles on a CUDA GPU with denoising enabled. Use the reference resolution and aspect ratio for the final output; do not upscale a low-resolution render. Test renders may use a lower resolution.
8. Do not call external asset or generation services. Do not read, reuse, or modify other tasks' outputs. Do not read evaluation-side annotations, ground truth, evaluation clips, question banks, or answers.
9. Every reconstructed frame must correspond to the same time in the original video, matching the subject state, action phase, interaction, and camera composition at that moment. Do not hide discrepancies using temporal shifts, speed changes, frame interpolation, or repeated frames.
10. Strictly match subjects' image-left/image-right positions, orientations, motion directions, and interactions. Distinguish image directions from subjects' anatomical directions and maintain correct correspondence across camera changes. Do not mirror or reverse left and right.

4. Check that the specific objects' visible silhouettes, structures, components, local appearance, and action details are nearly identical, rather than merely sharing categories.
